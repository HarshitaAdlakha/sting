The problem

High prices are easy to see. Agreement is hard to prove.

  • LLM pricing agents can sustain prices above the competitive level (Fish et al. 2024; Agrawal et al. 2025).
  • Parallel high prices can come from shared incentives, not agreement. Courts ask for "plus factors".
  • Regulators use stings, and an unaccepted invitation to collude is itself unlawful (FTC v. Valassis, 2006).
  • The sprint brief: "a trusted agent that offers to collude has not yet been tested."

Takeaways

A cheap probe with clear blind spots

For auditors
  • Test with indirect signals, not only explicit offers
  • Don't treat refusals or compliance language as competitive conduct
  • Channel design matters: words carried the collusion
Limitations
  • Stylised 3-firm market, 16 rounds, 8 seeds per cell
  • One provider; price effect measurable for one model
  • LLM judge, hand-checked on 24 transcripts
Dual-use and ethics discussion: report, Appendix A8 / 9
